# MASTER PROMPT: BUILD AN ULTRA-REALISTIC MOTORCYCLE RIDING EXPERIENCE IN DA NANG 3D

## 1. PROJECT CONTEXT

You are working on an existing 3D web experience:

https://danangbridges.netlify.app/

Project name: **Da Nang 3D – Explore the Bridges of Da Nang, Vietnam.**

The website allows visitors to explore Da Nang through an interactive 3D environment featuring famous landmarks and bridges, including:

- Dragon Bridge (Cầu Rồng).
- Han River Bridge (Cầu Sông Hàn).
- Tran Thi Ly Bridge (Cầu Trần Thị Lý).
- Thuan Phuoc Bridge (Cầu Thuận Phước).
- Other surrounding roads, buildings, river landscapes and city environments.

Existing experiences include:

- Paragliding.
- Car driving.
- Walking exploration.
- Guided tours and cinematic camera experiences.

The current interface already has a **Motorcycle / Xe máy** option marked as coming soon.

Your mission is to turn this placeholder into a fully functional, visually spectacular and immersive motorcycle riding experience.

### Most important objective

Do NOT create an independent motorcycle simulator or rebuild the existing website.

Integrate motorcycle riding as a native transportation mode within the existing 3D world.

The experience should make visitors feel as though they are genuinely riding a motorcycle through the streets and across the iconic bridges of Da Nang.

The result must be significantly more immersive than simply attaching a motorcycle model to a moving camera.

## 3. EXPERIENCE DESIGN – MOTORCYCLE RIDING

The motorcycle mode should feel like a real-world riding experience, not an arcade racing game.

The primary experience is free exploration of Da Nang.

Visitors should be able to ride through existing roads, explore the surroundings and cross the city's iconic bridges.

### 3.1 Motorcycle model

Create or integrate a highly realistic motorcycle model.

The preferred initial vehicle is a modern Vietnamese-style automatic scooter.

The motorcycle should have:

- Realistic body proportions.
- Detailed front and rear wheels.
- Realistic tires and tire textures.
- Functional-looking headlights and taillights.
- Turn signals.
- Mirrors.
- Handlebars.
- Brake components.
- Suspension.
- Seat and body panels.
- Realistic surface materials and reflections.

Avoid generic low-poly vehicle models.

Avoid recognizable manufacturer logos unless the project already has properly usable assets.

The motorcycle should look convincing from both first-person and third-person camera angles.

The model must be optimized for real-time WebGL rendering.

### 3.2 Rider and character animation

The motorcycle must have a convincing rider.

The rider should:

- Sit naturally on the motorcycle.
- Keep hands connected to the handlebars.
- Position feet appropriately.
- Lean naturally when turning.
- React to acceleration and braking.
- Maintain believable body posture while moving.

Avoid floating hands, disconnected limbs, clipping through the motorcycle or unnatural poses.

If the project already supports character selection or player identity, reuse the existing system.

Do not create a conflicting character management system.

If the experience supports multiplayer, other visitors must see a coherent motorcycle and rider rather than an invisible vehicle or an unrelated character.

### 3.3 First-person camera – The primary experience

The most important camera should create the feeling of sitting on the motorcycle.

The visitor should feel physically connected to the vehicle.

Include, where appropriate:

- Visible handlebars.
- Front motorcycle bodywork.
- Dashboard or instrument panel.
- Rear-view mirrors.
- Natural road perspective.
- Accurate sense of height above the road.
- Realistic perspective and field of view.

The camera must respond naturally to steering and acceleration.

When turning, the motorcycle and camera should exhibit believable lean behavior.

Add subtle physical feedback:

- Suspension response.
- Road vibration.
- Slight movement when accelerating.
- Natural camera inertia.
- Small environmental vibrations at high speeds.

Avoid excessive camera shake, unnatural movement or motion sickness.

### 3.4 Third-person camera

Provide an optional third-person follow camera.

The camera should follow the motorcycle smoothly while maintaining a cinematic viewing angle.

It should:

- Track the motorcycle without excessive lag.
- Avoid clipping through buildings and surrounding geometry.
- Maintain a sensible distance.
- React smoothly to turning.
- Show the rider and motorcycle clearly.

Allow switching between first-person and third-person views without interrupting the ride.

## 4. REALISTIC MOTORCYCLE PHYSICS

This is one of the most important parts of the experience.

The motorcycle must behave like an actual road vehicle.

Implement realistic and responsive:

- Acceleration.
- Deceleration.
- Steering.
- Braking.
- Turning radius.
- Leaning when cornering.
- Suspension movement.
- Wheel rotation.
- Tire-road interaction.
- Weight and inertia.
- Speed-dependent steering sensitivity.

Avoid instantaneous changes in speed or direction.

The motorcycle should feel heavier at higher speeds and more responsive at lower speeds.

When accelerating, the vehicle should exhibit subtle weight transfer.

When braking, the vehicle should decelerate progressively rather than stopping instantly.

When turning, the motorcycle should lean naturally instead of rotating like a rigid object.

The physics should be believable but still enjoyable for casual visitors.

This is a sightseeing experience, not a demanding professional motorcycle simulator.

### Road interaction

The motorcycle must remain physically connected to the road surface.

Pay particular attention to:

- Road elevation changes.
- Bridge ramps.
- Slopes.
- Uneven road geometry.
- Road boundaries.
- Curbs.
- Bridge barriers.
- Road intersections.
- Different road surfaces.

Prevent the motorcycle from accidentally driving across sidewalks, floating above roads or falling through bridge geometry.

Implement appropriate collision and recovery behavior.

If the motorcycle leaves a valid driving surface, provide a natural recovery mechanism rather than leaving the visitor stuck indefinitely.

## 5. DA NANG ENVIRONMENT INTEGRATION

This feature must showcase the existing Da Nang environment.

The motorcycle must travel within the same 3D world as the other transportation modes.

### Important landmarks

Ensure the experience integrates naturally with existing locations:

- Cầu Rồng.
- Cầu Sông Hàn.
- Cầu Trần Thị Lý.
- Cầu Thuận Phước.
- Surrounding roads and riverfront areas.
- Existing city buildings and landmarks.

Do not create disconnected duplicate bridge environments.

Reuse actual existing scene geometry and road infrastructure whenever possible.

Inspect whether the road network currently connects these areas sufficiently for continuous motorcycle riding.

If there are missing or incomplete road connections, identify them in the specification and propose the smallest viable improvements.

Do not rebuild the entire city.

### Road realism

The road surface must look convincing from the rider's perspective.

Improve or reuse:

- Asphalt textures.
- Road markings.
- Lane separation.
- Directional arrows.
- Crosswalks.
- Roadside curbs.
- Streetlights.
- Traffic signs.
- Sidewalks.
- Roadside barriers.
- Drainage and environmental details where appropriate.

Ensure the motorcycle follows the correct Vietnamese right-hand traffic orientation.

Avoid placing road markings or street furniture arbitrarily.

### Environmental realism

Preserve the existing city atmosphere.

The visitor should be able to appreciate:

- The Han River.
- Bridge architecture.
- Riverfront buildings.
- City skylines.
- Street lighting.
- Surrounding traffic.
- Reflections from the water.

The scenery should remain visually coherent when observed at motorcycle speed.

---

## 6. TRAFFIC AND LIVING CITY

A realistic motorcycle experience needs a living environment.

Reuse existing vehicle and traffic systems whenever possible.

Other vehicles should:

- Follow appropriate road directions.
- Move at believable speeds.
- Maintain reasonable spacing.
- Respect road geometry.
- Avoid unrealistic movement.
- Avoid appearing suddenly in front of the player.

Include realistic ambient city movement where supported by existing systems.

However, traffic must not constantly block the visitor or make the experience frustrating.

The objective is to create the feeling of riding through an active Vietnamese city.

The motorcycle should coexist naturally with the existing cars, traffic and other visitors.

Do not turn this feature into a full traffic simulation project.

Prioritize the motorcycle experience itself.

## 7. AUDIO DESIGN – EXTREMELY IMPORTANT

Audio must be designed as an integral part of the experience.

The motorcycle should not sound like a single looping engine recording.

Build a layered and responsive audio experience.

### Motorcycle audio

Include:

- Engine idling.
- Engine acceleration.
- Engine sound changing with RPM and speed.
- Engine deceleration.
- Tire contact with asphalt.
- Subtle suspension and road vibration sounds.
- Brake sounds where appropriate.
- Horn.
- Turn signal indicators.
- Starting and stopping sounds.

The engine audio must dynamically respond to acceleration and deceleration.

Avoid abrupt audio transitions, repetitive loops that are easy to recognize, distorted audio and sudden volume spikes.

### Environmental audio

Include spatial environmental sounds such as:

- Wind that becomes stronger as the motorcycle accelerates.
- Traffic passing nearby.
- Other motorcycles.
- Cars and horns.
- City ambience.
- River ambience.
- Bridge-specific environmental atmosphere.

Audio positioning should change naturally based on the motorcycle's location.

When entering an enclosed environment such as an existing tunnel, reuse or implement appropriate acoustic transitions if feasible.

Make audio transitions smooth and avoid overlapping or leaking sounds after switching modes.

Provide independent sound controls following the existing website's audio conventions.

## 8. WEATHER AND TIME-OF-DAY INTEGRATION

One of the project's existing strengths is its interactive weather and lighting system.

The motorcycle mode must integrate with it.

Do not build an entirely separate weather system.

### Daytime

- Natural sunlight.
- Realistic road shading.
- Proper reflections on motorcycle surfaces.
- Realistic environmental lighting.

### Nighttime

- Functional motorcycle headlights.
- Rear lights.
- Instrument panel illumination.
- Streetlight reflections.
- Realistic nighttime city atmosphere.
- Appropriate exposure and visibility.

### Rain

Rain should dramatically change the riding experience.

Include where supported:

- Wet asphalt.
- Reflections of streetlights and buildings.
- Reflections from motorcycle headlights.
- Tire spray.
- Rain particles.
- Ambient rain sound.
- Appropriate camera and environmental effects.
- Subtle changes to road traction.

Avoid unrealistic mirror-like reflections across every surface.

The motorcycle must remain controllable in adverse weather.

The existing storm and weather scenarios should remain compatible with this new mode where practical.

Inspect the existing car and walking control implementations before defining the final motorcycle controls.

Reuse established input conventions wherever practical.

Suggested desktop controls:

| D / Right Arrow | Steer right |

The final control mapping should account for the existing vehicle control system and avoid conflicts with camera controls.

### Mobile support

Design an appropriate touch interface if the project's existing mobile capabilities allow it.

Include:

- Acceleration control.
- Brake control.
- Steering controls.
- Camera view switching.
- Exit button.
- Optional horn control.

Touch controls must be comfortable, responsive and visually integrated with the experience.

Do not let the controls obscure too much of the 3D environment.

Handle different screen sizes and mobile safe areas.

## 10. MOTORCYCLE UI / HUD

Create a clean, immersive and unobtrusive interface.

The HUD should support riding rather than distract from the environment.

Consider:

- Speedometer.
- Gear or automatic transmission indication if appropriate.
- Engine status.
- Headlight status.
- Camera switching.
- Horn.
- Brake and accelerator controls.
- Exit experience.

Use a visual style consistent with the existing Da Nang 3D website.

The speedometer should respond to actual vehicle speed, not a decorative animation.

The HUD should be available in the existing website languages using the established localization system.

Do not introduce a completely different design language.

## 11. MULTIPLAYER AND SHARED-WORLD COMPATIBILITY

Inspect the current shared-world architecture before designing this functionality.

If the project supports multiple visitors in a shared environment:

- Other visitors should see the motorcycle and its rider.
- Motorcycle position and orientation should synchronize correctly.
- Movement should appear smooth for remote visitors.
- Rider identity should remain consistent with the existing system.
- Vehicle transitions should not leave stale avatars or duplicate vehicles.
- Exiting motorcycle mode should cleanly update the shared world.

Reuse the current networking and synchronization mechanisms.

Do not introduce an independent multiplayer implementation.

If multiplayer synchronization is not currently supported for vehicle modes, identify the actual limitation and propose an implementation that fits the existing architecture.

The single-player experience must still work when the shared-world server is unavailable, following the project's existing offline fallback behavior.

## 12. PERFORMANCE AND TECHNICAL QUALITY

This is a browser-based real-time 3D application.

Visual quality must not come at the cost of unusable performance.

Pay particular attention to:

- Draw calls.
- Polygon count.
- Texture memory.
- Shadow rendering.
- Dynamic lighting.
- Reflection calculations.
- Particle systems.
- Audio processing.
- Animation updates.
- Physics calculations.
- Network update frequency.

Reuse existing rendering infrastructure.

Use appropriate level of detail, frustum culling, instancing, resource reuse and optimized asset loading when supported by the existing architecture.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies.

Do not add expensive post-processing effects merely to make screenshots look impressive.

Make sure that:

- GPU resources are disposed correctly.
- Event listeners are cleaned up.
- Animation loops do not duplicate.
- Audio is stopped or released when leaving the mode.
- Switching between transportation modes does not cause memory leaks.
- The motorcycle does not negatively affect the existing car, paragliding or walking modes.

Establish and compare performance against the existing project baseline.

Target smooth performance on capable desktop devices and stable performance on reasonably capable mobile devices.

Provide graceful degradation when hardware cannot support the highest visual settings.
