# TASK: INVESTIGATE & FIX INTRO GUIDE AUDIO STUTTERING, LAG AND CRACKLING

**Project:** https://danangbridges.netlify.app/

**Role:** Act as a Senior Audio Engineer, Three.js Performance Engineer, Frontend Architect and QA Engineer.

**Execution mode:** Use Claude Superpowers to investigate, plan, implement and verify the fix. Work autonomously and make technically sound decisions based on the existing project architecture.

---

## 1. PROBLEM STATEMENT

The current Intro Guide has a serious audio-quality problem, particularly toward the final segment of the introduction.

Observed symptoms:

- Audio becomes choppy and intermittently stutters.
- Audio produces crackling, distortion or unpleasant digital noise.
- Audio playback becomes delayed or temporarily freezes.
- Audio is not smooth and consistent during the final portion of the introduction.
- The issue appears more noticeable toward the end rather than throughout the entire introduction.

**This is a quality issue that must be fixed, not something to work around.**

The primary objective is to ensure that the entire Intro Guide has smooth, continuous, clean and synchronized audio from beginning to end.

Do not assume that the audio file itself is necessarily the problem. Investigate the complete playback pipeline and identify the actual root cause.

## 2. USE SUPERPOWERS TO INVESTIGATE BEFORE IMPLEMENTING

Follow the appropriate Superpowers workflow.

### Phase 1: Understand the existing architecture

Before changing any code:

1. Review the entire project structure.
2. Locate the Intro Guide implementation and identify all related components, modules, services and assets.
3. Understand how the introduction is orchestrated, including:
   - Intro animation and scene transitions.
   - Background music.
   - Narration / voice-over.
   - Sound effects.
   - Audio playback controls.
   - Audio loading and asset preloading.
   - Pause, resume, replay and skip behavior.
   - Final-segment transition and cleanup logic.
4. Trace the audio lifecycle from initial loading to the final segment.
5. Identify how Three.js rendering, animations and audio playback interact.

Do not create a new audio architecture unnecessarily. First understand and reuse the existing implementation wherever possible.

### Phase 2: Reproduce and diagnose the issue

Reproduce the issue locally using the actual Intro Guide.

Pay special attention to the final 20–30% of the introduction and the transitions immediately before and after the problematic segment.

Investigate the following potential causes:

**A. Audio playback problems**
- Audio buffer decoding delays.
- Incorrect or inefficient audio loading strategy.
- Audio files being loaded too late.
- Audio playback restarting unexpectedly.
- Multiple audio instances playing simultaneously.
- Incorrect audio scheduling or timing.
- Audio source switching at segment boundaries.
- Incorrect audio node lifecycle management.
- AudioContext being suspended, recreated or interrupted.
- Audio synchronization depending incorrectly on animation timing.

**B. Audio quality problems**
- Audio clipping or excessive gain.
- Distortion caused by overlapping narration, music and sound effects.
- Inconsistent volume levels between audio tracks.
- Incorrect sample rate, encoding or corrupted audio assets.
- Poor-quality audio conversion or compression.
- Incorrect gain management during transitions.

**C. Three.js and browser performance problems**
- Excessive rendering workload during the final segment.
- High CPU or GPU usage.
- Expensive animations, shaders, particles or post-processing.
- Memory leaks and excessive memory allocation.
- Garbage collection pauses.
- Main-thread blocking tasks.
- Long-running JavaScript operations.
- Unnecessary event listeners or animation loops.
- Heavy asset loading during audio playback.

**D. Transition and lifecycle problems**
- Previous audio not being properly stopped or disposed of.
- Multiple timers or callbacks triggering the same playback.
- Incorrect timing when transitioning between introduction segments.
- Audio cleanup occurring too early or too late.
- Repeated creation of audio resources.
- Audio playback being interrupted by scene initialization or destruction.

Do not assume that all these problems exist. Use actual evidence from the code, browser diagnostics and reproduction tests to determine which ones are responsible.

### Phase 3: Root Cause Analysis

Before implementing changes:

1. Identify the exact component, function or process causing the problem.
2. Determine why the issue becomes noticeable near the end of the introduction.
3. Distinguish between audio decoding problems, playback scheduling issues, audio clipping and rendering-performance bottlenecks.
4. Check browser Console errors and warnings.
5. Inspect Network requests, asset loading times and audio resource availability.
6. Profile CPU, memory, frame rate and main-thread activity during the problematic segment.
7. Compare audio behavior between the beginning, middle and end of the introduction.

Produce a concise technical diagnosis and a concrete implementation plan.

Do not jump directly to random code modifications.

---

## 3. IMPLEMENT THE FIX

Once the root cause has been identified, implement the smallest reliable solution that fixes the underlying problem.

Consider the following engineering requirements where applicable.

### A. Stable audio playback

- Avoid unnecessary audio resource recreation.
- Avoid restarting or reloading audio during segment transitions.
- Ensure audio assets are available before the segments that require them.
- Prevent simultaneous playback of unintended duplicate audio instances.
- Ensure playback timing does not depend on unstable animation frame timing.
- Manage audio lifecycle and cleanup correctly.
- Ensure pause and resume do not introduce timing errors.
- Prevent audio playback from being interrupted by unrelated scene updates.

If the existing implementation uses Web Audio API, review AudioContext, AudioBuffer, AudioBufferSourceNode, GainNode and their lifecycle carefully.

If it uses HTMLAudioElement, review preload behavior, media readiness, playback promises, buffering, source reassignment and event handling.

Use the approach that best fits the existing architecture. Do not migrate the entire audio system without strong technical justification.

### B. Clean and distortion-free audio

- Prevent audio clipping.
- Maintain appropriate gain levels.
- Avoid unwanted overlap between narration, background music and sound effects.
- Preserve clear and natural voice-over quality.
- Ensure smooth volume transitions where appropriate.
- Avoid sudden volume jumps, clicks, pops or crackling.

Do not simply reduce the master volume to hide distortion.

### C. Smooth transitions

Pay special attention to the final segment.

- Ensure transitions between audio tracks are seamless.
- Prevent abrupt audio interruptions.
- Prevent duplicate playback during transitions.
- Avoid unnecessary source switching.
- Maintain correct synchronization between narration, music and visual content.

### D. Rendering and performance optimization

If profiling confirms that Three.js rendering is contributing to audio interruptions:

- Optimize expensive rendering operations.
- Reduce unnecessary allocations during animation.
- Avoid blocking the main thread.
- Optimize unnecessary animation and effect updates.
- Ensure proper cleanup of temporary objects and resources.
- Avoid introducing additional performance overhead.

Do not unnecessarily reduce the visual quality of the Intro Guide.

The objective is to improve performance while preserving the existing visual experience.

---

## 4. REGRESSION TESTING

A code change is not sufficient evidence that the problem is fixed.

Test the actual Intro Guide from beginning to end.

### Required test scenarios

1. Start the introduction from the first segment and allow it to play uninterrupted until completion.
2. Carefully listen to the final 20–30% of the introduction.
3. Replay the introduction multiple times.
4. Pause and resume playback at different segments.
5. Use the previous/next playback controls where applicable.
6. Test the skip and replay behavior.
7. Test the supported language versions, including Vietnamese and English.
8. Verify that narration, background music and sound effects do not interfere with one another.
9. Verify that audio playback remains stable while the Three.js scene is rendering complex animations.
10. Check that finishing or exiting the Intro Guide properly releases audio resources.

Use automated browser tests and available profiling tools where appropriate.

Also perform actual listening tests. Automated tests alone cannot reliably determine whether sound is natural, smooth or free from audible distortion.

Test on the primary desktop browser and check other supported browsers where feasible.

### Performance verification

Compare the problematic segment before and after the fix.

Inspect:

- Audio playback interruptions.
- Audio loading and decoding behavior.
- JavaScript long tasks.
- CPU and memory usage.
- Frame rate and frame-time spikes.
- Browser Console errors.
- Duplicate audio playback.
- Unhandled audio playback promises.

Do not claim success based solely on the absence of JavaScript errors.

---

## 5. STRICT ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The task is considered complete only when ALL the following conditions are satisfied.

| Requirement | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Audio smoothness | Continuous and smooth playback from beginning to end |
| Audio stuttering | No audible stuttering or intermittent interruptions |
| Audio distortion | No crackling, clipping, popping or unpleasant digital noise |
| Playback latency | No unexpected delay or sluggish playback |
| Synchronization | Narration, music and visual content remain appropriately synchronized |
| Final segment | Plays as smoothly as the beginning and middle segments |
| Transitions | No unwanted interruption, duplicate audio or sudden volume changes |
| Replay | Replaying the introduction does not reproduce the issue |
| Pause / Resume | Audio resumes correctly without glitches or timing problems |
| Visual experience | Existing Intro Guide visuals and animations remain intact |
| Browser stability | No new Console errors, unhandled playback failures or resource leaks |

**Most important acceptance condition:**

Listening to the entire Intro Guide, especially the final segment, must feel natural and uninterrupted.

There must be:
- NO stuttering.
- NO lag.
- NO unexpected playback delay.
- NO crackling.
- NO clipping.
- NO abrupt audio cuts.
- NO unpleasant sound distortion.

The audio quality must remain consistent throughout the entire introduction.

---

## 6. IMPORTANT RESTRICTIONS

Do NOT:

- Simply lower the audio volume to hide the problem.
- Remove the problematic final segment.
- Shorten the introduction to avoid the issue.
- Disable background music or narration as a workaround.
- Reduce visual quality unnecessarily.
- Replace the entire Intro Guide with a different implementation without justification.
- Introduce additional audio libraries without first evaluating the existing system.
- Make speculative modifications without understanding the root cause.
- Claim the issue is fixed without testing actual audio playback.

Preserve the current website experience, features, content, visual identity and existing audio content unless a specific asset is demonstrated to be defective.

All changes must be compatible with the existing project architecture.

---

## 7. FINAL DELIVERABLE

After implementation, provide a technical summary containing:

1. **Root cause:** What exactly caused the audio problem?
2. **Affected components:** Which files, functions or systems were responsible?
3. **Implemented fixes:** What changes were made and why?
4. **Performance improvements:** What bottlenecks were resolved?
5. **Testing results:** Which scenarios were tested, and what were the actual results?
6. **Remaining issues:** Clearly disclose anything that could not be reproduced, tested or verified.

Do not report an acceptance criterion as passed unless it has actually been verified.

## FINAL DIRECTIVE

Take ownership of this issue from investigation through implementation and verification.

Use Superpowers to plan and execute the work systematically. Make reasonable technical decisions autonomously, following the existing architecture rather than asking me to choose implementation details.

**The priority is NOT to make the code look cleaner. The priority is to eliminate the actual audio defects and deliver a consistently smooth, clean and uninterrupted Intro Guide experience.**

Do not stop after identifying the issue or writing a plan. Execute the implementation and complete the regression tests.
