# TASK: BUILD A CINEMATIC 3D INTRO GUIDE FOR DA NANG BRIDGES

**Live Project:** https://danangbridges.netlify.app/

## 1. ROLE & OBJECTIVE

Act as a Senior Three.js Engineer, Creative Director, Cinematic Experience Designer, and UI/UX Specialist.

Your mission is to design and implement a spectacular, immersive, realistic 3D Intro Guide for the existing Da Nang Bridges website.

This is NOT a conventional onboarding tutorial, a collection of static slides, or a modal containing descriptions and icons.

I want visitors to feel as if they are watching a beautifully directed cinematic introduction to an interactive virtual Da Nang before entering the actual experience.

The introduction must visually demonstrate the actual features that already exist in the project using the real 3D environment, existing models, camera systems, animations, lighting, and effects.

**Core principle: Show the experience, don't just describe it.**

A visitor should immediately understand:
- What they can explore.
- Which transportation and exploration modes are available.
- How realistic and interactive the environment is.
- What spectacular landmarks and events they can experience.
- How to start their own journey.

## 2. FIRST: DEEPLY UNDERSTAND THE EXISTING PROJECT

Before implementation, thoroughly inspect the live website and the complete project source code.

Review:
- Existing Three.js scene and rendering architecture.
- Scene initialization and lifecycle.
- Camera systems and available camera positions.
- Existing bridge models and animations.
- Environment, lighting, day/night transitions, and shadows.
- Existing Dragon Bridge fire and water effects.
- Han River Bridge rotation mechanism.
- Paragliding, car, motorcycle, and walking implementations.
- Existing mode-selection screens and user onboarding.
- Existing UI components, language system, and interaction patterns.
- Asset loading, performance optimization, and multiplayer functionality.

The project already has these primary experiences:

1. **Paragliding:** Free exploration above the Han River.
2. **Car:** Driving a VinFast VF3 through the bridges.
3. **Motorcycle:** Riding a scooter through Da Nang.
4. **Walking:** Walking along the bridges and both riverbanks.
5. **Day & Night:** Switching between daytime and nighttime environments.
6. **Dragon Bridge Show:** Experiencing the Dragon Bridge breathing fire and spraying water.
7. **Han River Bridge Rotation:** Watching and experiencing the rotating bridge mechanism.
8. **Guided Exploration:** Automated tours, viewpoints, and narration.
9. **Weather Effects:** Existing weather settings and storm scenarios.

These are the actual project experiences, not suggestions to build new ones.

IMPORTANT:
- Do not invent features that do not exist.
- Do not create fake demonstrations that misrepresent actual functionality.
- Do not replace existing 3D models with unrelated stock images or generic illustrations.
- Do not rebuild existing mechanics just to create the introduction.
- Reuse existing implementations wherever possible.
- If a feature has limitations, present those limitations honestly.

The entire introduction must feel native to the existing application.

## 3. CREATIVE DIRECTION

Create a premium cinematic experience inspired by modern interactive 3D showcases, immersive travel experiences, and high-end game introductions.

Visual characteristics:

- Photorealistic 3D environment.
- Smooth cinematic camera movements.
- Beautiful depth and atmospheric perspective.
- Natural daylight, realistic nighttime illumination.
- Smooth transitions between scenes.
- Dynamic water reflections.
- Beautiful lighting and subtle bloom where appropriate.
- Realistic fire, water, and environmental effects.
- Minimal but premium typography.
- Elegant animated text and UI elements.
- Carefully synchronized visual transitions.

Avoid:
- Generic dashboard designs.
- Excessive cards and unnecessary UI panels.
- Large blocks of descriptive text.
- Cartoonish effects.
- Overly bright neon interfaces.
- Unnecessary loading animations.
- Static screenshots pretending to be interactive 3D scenes.

The real 3D world must remain the visual centerpiece.

## 4. INTRODUCTION EXPERIENCE — CINEMATIC STORYBOARD

Design an approximately 45–60 second cinematic introduction, with flexible timing depending on scene loading and animation complexity.

The introduction should consist of the following sequences.

### SCENE 01 — WELCOME TO DA NANG

**Duration:** 5–7 seconds.

Open with a breathtaking cinematic aerial view of the actual Da Nang 3D environment.

Camera direction:
- Begin with a high-altitude view.
- Slowly descend toward the Han River.
- Reveal the city skyline and its bridges.
- Showcase the Dragon Bridge, Han River Bridge, and surrounding landmarks.
- Use smooth cinematic camera movement.
- Create an immediate feeling of depth and scale.

Visual atmosphere:
- Beautiful daylight.
- Natural sunlight and shadows.
- Realistic water reflections.
- Subtle atmospheric depth.

Typography:

Main title:
"DISCOVER DA NANG"

Subtitle:
"An interactive journey through the city of bridges."

Vietnamese version:
"KHÁM PHÁ ĐÀ NẴNG"

"Thành phố bên sông Hàn – Một hành trình, vô vàn góc nhìn."

The text should appear elegantly without covering the main scenery.

### SCENE 02 — FOUR WAYS TO EXPLORE

**Duration:** 14–18 seconds.

This is one of the most important scenes.

Introduce the four actual exploration modes through cinematic first-person or third-person demonstrations.

Do not simply display four static cards.

Each mode should temporarily become the main visual focus, with a camera transition into its corresponding actual environment.

**A. PARAGLIDING**

Show the actual paragliding experience.

Visual direction:
- First-person aerial perspective.
- Smooth forward movement above the Han River.
- Reveal bridges and city scenery from the sky.
- Show the sensation of free flight.
- Use realistic camera motion and depth.

Overlay:
"PARAGLIDING"

"Fly freely above the Han River."

Vietnamese:
"DÙ LƯỢN – Bay tự do trên sông Hàn."

**B. CAR DRIVING**

Show the actual VinFast VF3 driving experience.

Visual direction:
- Use the existing VF3 model.
- Show the actual road and bridge environment.
- Demonstrate the vehicle moving forward.
- Switch naturally between available camera perspectives.
- Give the visitor the feeling of driving through Da Nang.

Overlay:
"CAR DRIVING"

"Explore Da Nang behind the wheel."

Vietnamese:
"Ô TÔ – Lái xe khám phá những cây cầu."

**C. MOTORCYCLE RIDING**

Show the existing motorcycle/scooter experience.

Visual direction:
- Use the actual motorcycle model.
- Demonstrate realistic movement across the existing roads.
- Show a suitable riding camera perspective.
- Capture the feeling of travelling freely through the city.

Overlay:
"MOTORCYCLE"

"Ride through the city at your own pace."

Vietnamese:
"XE MÁY – Tự do rong ruổi qua từng cây cầu."

**D. WALKING**

Show the actual walking experience.

Visual direction:
- First-person or existing walking camera perspective.
- Walk naturally along the bridge or riverside.
- Show the environment from human eye level.
- Demonstrate the feeling of exploring the city at a relaxed pace.

Overlay:
"WALKING"

"Slow down and discover every detail."

Vietnamese:
"ĐI DẠO – Chậm lại và cảm nhận Đà Nẵng."

Important implementation requirements:
- Use actual existing mode implementations.
- Use existing character and vehicle models.
- Do not create separate fake vehicles or duplicate scenes.
- If a mode is only supported on desktop, explicitly preserve that limitation in the guide.
- Keep the demonstrations short and visually distinct.

### SCENE 03 — EXPERIENCE THE CITY, DAY AND NIGHT

**Duration:** 6–8 seconds.

Showcase the existing day/night system.

Camera direction:
- Start with a beautiful daytime aerial view.
- Gradually transition toward sunset.
- Allow the sky to become darker naturally.
- City lights illuminate progressively.
- Bridge lights turn on.
- Reflections appear across the Han River.
- Finish with a spectacular nighttime panoramic view.

The transition should demonstrate the actual day/night implementation, not simply crossfade between two static backgrounds.

Overlay:

"ONE CITY. TWO WORLDS."

"Experience Da Nang from sunrise to nightfall."

Vietnamese:

"MỘT THÀNH PHỐ – HAI SẮC THÁI."

"Khám phá Đà Nẵng từ ban ngày đến đêm rực rỡ."

Where practical, briefly showcase the existing environmental/weather controls as an additional capability without distracting from the main sequence.

### SCENE 04 — THE DRAGON COMES ALIVE

**Duration:** 6–8 seconds.

This must be a visual highlight of the entire introduction.

Show the actual Dragon Bridge and its existing fire/water demonstration.

Suggested cinematic direction:

1. Camera approaches the Dragon Bridge at night.
2. Slowly moves toward the dragon's head.
3. Bridge lighting creates a dramatic atmosphere.
4. The dragon breathes fire.
5. Fire illuminates the surrounding scene and river.
6. Transition into the water-spraying effect.
7. Capture the water particles and lighting reflections.

Use the actual existing Dragon Bridge effects and animation systems.

Do not fake the event using a static fire image or video.

Overlay:

"THE DRAGON AWAKENS"

"Witness the legendary fire and water show."

Vietnamese:

"KHI RỒNG THIÊNG THỨC GIẤC"

"Chiêm ngưỡng màn trình diễn phun lửa và nước."

This scene should feel spectacular, powerful, and memorable.

### SCENE 05 — THE ROTATING HAN RIVER BRIDGE

**Duration:** 6–8 seconds.

Demonstrate the actual Han River Bridge rotation mechanism.

Suggested camera direction:
- Start with a beautiful nighttime side view.
- Slowly approach the bridge.
- Showcase the central rotating span.
- Demonstrate the actual rotation animation.
- Use a secondary aerial or elevated perspective if available.
- Reveal the river beneath the opening bridge.
- Capture the illuminated bridge and river reflections.

Overlay:

"ENGINEERING IN MOTION"

"Experience the iconic rotating Han River Bridge."

Vietnamese:

"KỲ QUAN CHUYỂN ĐỘNG"

"Khám phá cơ chế quay độc đáo của cầu sông Hàn."

Important:
- Use the real existing bridge rotation logic.
- Preserve the actual rotation axis and mechanism.
- Do not invent a different bridge movement.
- Respect the application's existing state and trigger conditions.

### SCENE 06 — YOUR JOURNEY BEGINS HERE

**Duration:** 5–7 seconds.

Return to a breathtaking aerial view of the entire river and bridge system.

Camera gently pulls back to reveal the complete environment.

Present a premium final interface.

Main title:

"YOUR DA NANG ADVENTURE STARTS NOW"

Vietnamese:

"HÀNH TRÌNH KHÁM PHÁ ĐÀ NẴNG BẮT ĐẦU"

Subtitle:

"Choose your way. Explore your city."

Vietnamese:

"Chọn cách khám phá của bạn."

Display four visually attractive choices:

- Dù lượn
- Ô tô
- Xe máy
- Đi dạo

Also provide access to the existing guided tour experience.

Primary CTA:

"START EXPLORING"

Vietnamese:

"BẮT ĐẦU KHÁM PHÁ"

When clicked, transition directly into the existing mode-selection and entry flow.

Do not create a second independent experience-selection system.

## 5. INTERACTIVE INTRO GUIDE — NOT JUST AN AUTOMATIC VIDEO

Although the intro should play like a cinematic sequence, visitors must remain in control.

Implement:

- Skip Intro.
- Next / Previous scene navigation.
- Pause / Resume.
- Scene progress indicator.
- Replay Intro.
- Start Exploring.
- Optional "Don't show again" preference.

The UI should be elegant, minimal, and unobtrusive.

Visitors should be able to:
- Watch the complete cinematic introduction.
- Jump directly to a particular feature.
- Skip the introduction immediately.
- Replay it later.

For feature-specific sections, provide small interactive exploration actions where technically appropriate.

For example:
- "Try Paragliding"
- "Drive Now"
- "Explore on Foot"
- "Watch Dragon Show"
- "See Bridge Rotation"

Each action must navigate to or invoke the corresponding existing feature.

Avoid interrupting an active multiplayer session or modifying shared world state just to play an introduction.

## 6. SHOW REAL CONTROLS WHEN THEY MATTER

A good introduction should not only demonstrate the environment; it should help visitors understand how to interact with it.

Briefly introduce the actual controls for each mode.

Examples:
- Paragliding: movement, turning, and acceleration.
- Car: steering, acceleration, braking, and camera.
- Motorcycle: actual riding controls.
- Walking: WASD, Shift, mouse camera, and photo capture where available.

Read the actual control mappings from the source code.

Do not invent keyboard shortcuts.

Present controls visually using small keyboard-key elements rather than long paragraphs.

Only show the controls relevant to the currently demonstrated mode.

## 7. UI/UX AND VISUAL DESIGN

Create a premium cinematic interface.

Design direction:
- Dark translucent glass panels.
- Subtle gradients.
- Elegant typography.
- Small animated labels.
- Smooth fades and transitions.
- Sophisticated progress indicator.
- Beautiful hover and click feedback.
- Responsive layout.

The UI should look like a premium interactive travel experience, not a generic web application.

Keep the actual 3D scene visible at all times whenever possible.

Use screen positioning carefully:
- Main title: center or lower-center.
- Feature description: lower-left or lower-center.
- Progress: bottom edge.
- Navigation controls: subtle top or bottom corners.
- Skip button: clearly visible in the top-right corner.

Avoid excessive text.

## 8. TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

This is an existing Three.js application. Integration quality is critical.

Before coding:
- Understand the existing project structure.
- Identify reusable scene, camera, animation, and effect functions.
- Follow the existing architecture and coding conventions.
- Reuse the existing rendering pipeline.

Implementation requirements:

1. Do not initialize a second WebGL renderer unnecessarily.
2. Do not duplicate the entire 3D environment.
3. Avoid loading duplicate 3D assets.
4. Reuse actual cameras, models, lights, and animation systems.
5. Ensure all animation loops and event listeners are properly managed.
6. Restore the original camera, scene, and application state after the intro.
7. Do not leave temporary animations running in the background.
8. Avoid unnecessary memory allocations and GPU overhead.
9. Do not break existing multiplayer synchronization.
10. Do not block the application from becoming interactive.
11. Handle WebGL loading failures gracefully.
12. Keep the existing website fully functional when the intro is skipped or disabled.

The intro should not introduce a significant performance regression.

Where possible, implement cinematic camera sequences using the application's existing Three.js scene and rendering loop.

If certain existing effects cannot safely run during an introduction, implement a controlled preview using the existing visual assets and effect systems without affecting the real experience.

### First-time experience

- Display the Intro Guide when a visitor enters for the first time.
- Let visitors skip it immediately.
- Save the "Don't show again" preference using the project's existing persistence conventions.
- Provide a clear way to replay the intro.
- Respect the currently selected application language.
- Reuse the existing localization system rather than hardcoding a separate translation mechanism.

### Responsive behavior

Desktop:
- Full cinematic camera sequences.
- Full-screen 3D experience.
- Keyboard shortcuts.

Mobile:
- Adapt the interface to smaller screens.
- Do not expose desktop-only controls as if they were supported.
- Avoid heavy animations if device performance is limited.
- Preserve the actual supported interaction capabilities.

"I am not looking at an ordinary website. I am entering a living, breathing digital version of Da Nang."

The four exploration modes should make visitors excited to choose how they want to explore.

Build an introduction that makes people want to explore Da Nang immediately.
