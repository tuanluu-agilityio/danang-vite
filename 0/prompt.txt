# PROJECT: DA NANG RIVERSIDE REALISM UPGRADE
## Blender + Three.js | Focused Visual Enhancement

### 1. YOUR MISSION

You are a senior 3D Environment Artist, Blender Technical Director, Architectural Visualization Specialist, and Three.js Graphics Engineer.

Your task is to significantly improve the visual realism of an existing interactive 3D representation of Da Nang.

Existing website:

https://danangbridges.netlify.app/

The project already has an operational Three.js environment with existing bridges, camera systems, animations, weather, and interactive experiences.

For this iteration, you have ONLY TWO PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:

1. Rebuild and improve the Han River water environment.
2. Rebuild and improve the buildings and urban environment along both riverbanks.

The objective is to make the environment look substantially closer to real-world Da Nang.

Use Blender for professional 3D asset production and Three.js for real-time rendering.

IMPORTANT:

This is a focused visual upgrade, NOT a complete project rebuild.

Do not expand the project scope.

Do not modify existing bridge models or rewrite their animation systems.

Do not rebuild existing gameplay or camera controls.

Do not replace the existing Three.js architecture.

Preserve all working functionality.

# 3. OBJECTIVE A: PHOTOREALISTIC HAN RIVER

The Han River must become a visually convincing natural urban river.

This is the first major priority.

## 3.1 River Geometry

Inspect the current river mesh and determine whether it can be improved or should be replaced.

Requirements:

- Preserve the existing river location.
- Preserve its geographical direction.
- Preserve its approximate width.
- Follow the existing riverbanks.
- Maintain correct alignment with all existing bridges.
- Ensure correct alignment with existing piers and surrounding terrain.
- Avoid visible gaps between water and riverbanks.
- Avoid floating water surfaces.
- Avoid obvious intersections with bridge structures.

Do not arbitrarily change the geography of the environment.

Use Blender when necessary to create more detailed riverbank and terrain geometry.

However, keep the primary water surface compatible with the existing Three.js water rendering architecture.

## 3.2 Water Shader

Develop a realistic real-time water shader.

The desired water appearance should include:

- Multiple layers of subtle waves.
- Different wave frequencies.
- Natural wind-driven movement.
- Animated normal maps.
- Subtle surface displacement where appropriate.
- Realistic Fresnel effect.
- Depth-dependent water coloration.
- Natural sunlight reflections.
- Environmental reflections.
- Subtle variations in surface roughness.

The water must not look like:

- A flat blue plane.
- A swimming pool.
- A perfectly smooth mirror.
- An excessively transparent glass surface.
- An exaggerated ocean with huge waves.

The Han River should look like a relatively calm urban river with naturally moving water.

## 3.3 Reflections

Reflections are essential.

Implement a suitable reflection solution supported by the existing rendering architecture.

Evaluate:

- Environment-map reflections.
- Screen-space reflections.
- Planar reflections.
- Hybrid reflection techniques.

Select a suitable implementation based on visual quality and GPU cost.

At minimum, the water should visibly reflect:

- The sky.
- Nearby buildings.
- Bridge structures.
- Nighttime city lights.

Avoid reflection systems that significantly reduce FPS.

If full real-time reflections are too expensive, use a carefully designed approximation.

## 3.4 Water Behavior

The water must respond to environmental conditions.

DAYTIME:

- Natural blue-green coloration.
- Soft wave movement.
- Sunlight highlights.
- Environmental reflections.

SUNSET:

- Warm highlights.
- Sunset sky reflections.
- Natural color gradients.

NIGHT:

- Darker water.
- Reflections from buildings and street lights.
- Subtle moving highlights.

RAIN:

- Small rain-impact ripples.
- Increased surface roughness.
- Changes in reflection appearance.

STORM:

- Stronger wind-driven waves.
- More dynamic surface movement.
- Darker water appearance.

Preserve the existing weather controls.

Connect the new water system to the existing environment state instead of creating a separate weather controller.

## 3.5 Interaction With Existing Boats

Preserve existing boats and their movement systems.

Where appropriate, improve the visual interaction between boats and water.

Potential enhancements:

- Subtle boat wakes.
- Small water disturbances around moving boats.
- Improved visual contact with the water surface.

Do not rebuild the boat system as part of this iteration.

The second major priority is upgrading buildings along both sides of the Han River.

The current environment should no longer look like a collection of primitive blocks.

- Bach Dang Street.

- Riverside hotel buildings.
- Modern apartment buildings.
- Commercial buildings.
- Office buildings.
- Residential buildings.
- Riverside urban developments.

- Typical building heights.
- Building density.
- Architectural styles.
- Facade composition.
- Window distribution.
- Balcony patterns.
- Rooftop structures.
- Building colors.
- Street-level commercial architecture.

Do not create a generic international skyline.

Preserve the characteristic visual identity of Da Nang.

Use the existing project geography as the source of truth for building placement.

Do not invent a completely different city layout.

## 4.2 Blender Architectural Modeling

Use Blender to create a modular library of realistic buildings.

Do not model every building independently if a modular procedural workflow is more efficient.

Build several architectural families:

TYPE A — Modern Hotels

- Glass facades.
- Recessed balconies.
- Large windows.
- Rooftop details.
- Contemporary exterior materials.

TYPE B — Residential Buildings

- Multiple floors.
- Balconies.
- Window frames.
- Air-conditioning units.
- Exterior railings.
- Realistic rooftop structures.

TYPE C — Commercial Buildings

- Street-level storefronts.
- Glass entrances.
- Signage areas.
- Upper-floor facades.
- Varied window designs.

TYPE D — Office Buildings

- More uniform facade structures.
- Glass and concrete combinations.
- Modern architectural profiles.

TYPE E — Low-rise Vietnamese Buildings

- Narrow building footprints.
- Multiple floor configurations.
- Rooftop terraces.
- Balconies.
- Commercial ground floors.
- More traditional urban proportions.

Use these families to create visual diversity.

Avoid obvious repetition.

## 4.3 Building Geometry

Improve:

- Building silhouettes.
- Facades.
- Window frames.
- Balconies.
- Rooftop structures.
- Ground floors.
- Roof edges.
- Exterior architectural details.

Add geometry only when it has meaningful visual impact.

Use:

- Modular modeling.
- Beveled edges.
- Weighted normals.
- Clean topology.
- UV mapping.
- Procedural variations.

Avoid creating hundreds of unnecessary individual window meshes.

Use texture baking, instancing, and modular elements where appropriate.

## 4.4 PBR Materials

Create realistic materials for:

- Painted concrete.
- Exposed concrete.
- Glass.
- Aluminum.
- Steel.
- Stone.
- Ceramic tiles.
- Exterior paint.
- Rooftop surfaces.

Materials must have believable:

- Base color.
- Roughness.
- Metallic values.
- Normal detail.
- Ambient occlusion.

Avoid plastic-looking buildings.

Avoid excessively clean, identical facades.

Subtle imperfections and material variation are desirable.

## 4.5 Windows and Interior Lighting

Windows are extremely important for the nighttime scene.

Create convincing window materials.

During daytime:

- Appropriate glass reflections.
- Subtle interior visibility where useful.
- Natural variations between facades.

During nighttime:

- Some windows should be illuminated.
- Other windows should remain dark.
- Brightness should vary between buildings.
- Avoid making every window glow identically.

Use efficient emissive materials or shader-based techniques.

Do not add a separate expensive light source for every window.

## 4.6 Urban Environment Details

Improve the immediate surroundings of the buildings:

- Sidewalks.
- Riverside promenades.
- Trees.
- Decorative vegetation.
- Street furniture.
- Street lights.
- Small architectural elements.
- Building entrances.
- Roadside landscaping.

Keep this work focused on the existing riverside environment.

Do not expand into a complete road or traffic reconstruction project.

Preserve existing roads and traffic systems.

- Approximately 30 FPS or better on supported mid-range mobile hardware with appropriate quality settings.
- No significant memory leaks.
- No excessive texture loading.
- No unnecessary draw calls.
